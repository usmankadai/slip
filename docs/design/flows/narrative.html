Finish update: three low-fidelity layouts

Hierarchy only: boxes and labels, no identity. The numbered tags show the order the eye should take. A heavy border marks what leads. Boxed codes are activity chips: each one opens the activity.

The planner's question: "What do I tell the client?"
Most prominent: an editable narrative where each claim carries its activity chips.
Then: export; what was edited by hand versus generated.
Must not: let a claim exist without evidence.

Layouts drawn by Claude for Usman to choose between. The text is generated from the summary, trace and health results by templates (the phase plan says template-based first; the agent may polish it later). The planner then edits it.

Leads with
The narrative as one piece of writing, the way it will be read in the report.
Does well
Feels like writing, not filling in a form. The margin shows the evidence for whatever sentence the cursor is in, and keeps the counts of generated, edited and unsupported sentences in view.
Costs
Tracking which sentence is generated, edited or unsupported inside free text is the hardest thing on this page to build well. A planner who rewrites a paragraph freely can break the link between a sentence and its chips without noticing.

Leads with
One block per topic, in report order. Each block has its text, its chips beneath, and a mark saying whether it is generated or edited.
Does well
The rule "no claim without evidence" can be kept per block, so it is simple to build and simple to see. A planner can always get back to the generated text. A block that contradicts the data is caught and said in words.
Costs
Reads less like a finished document while editing. Evidence is per paragraph, not per sentence, so one chip row covers several claims. Splitting and joining paragraphs needs designing.

Leads with
A checklist of the facts Slip found, ticked by default for the important ones. The text is written from what is ticked.
Does well
Every claim starts from a fact with evidence, so nothing can enter without it. The planner decides what the client hears before worrying about wording. It is clear what was left out.
Costs
Two steps before there is a document. A planner's own point, not in Slip's list, has nowhere to start from. Once the text is edited, the ticks and the words can drift apart.

What all three share

Not a choice here
A generated claim always carries chips to the activities behind it. A claim the planner writes can stand without them, but is marked as their own statement, and a claim that contradicts the data is flagged in words, not hidden.
Edited and generated text are always told apart, on screen and in the export.
Export as Markdown first; PDF is the first thing the phase plan cuts if time is short. Chips become activity codes in the exported text.
Schedule content is rendered as text, never as HTML. Exported Markdown is escaped.
Open whichever is chosen
Where the narrative is kept between visits (the persistence spec says IndexedDB), and whether each edit goes in the audit log. Neither exists yet.
