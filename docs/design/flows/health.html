Health: three low-fidelity layouts

Hierarchy only: boxes and labels, no identity. The numbered tags show the order the eye should take. A heavy border marks what leads. The check names and thresholds are the real ones the engine uses; the figures are made up.

The planner's question: "Is this schedule getting better or worse?"
Most prominent: the checks that changed status or got worse since the last update, first.
Then: all 14 checks with before and after; click through to the activities behind each.
Must not: reduce everything to one score with no way in.

Layouts drawn by Claude for Usman to choose between. A filled square marks a check that fails; an empty one, a check that passes. Two of the 14 are reported only, with no pass or fail, and one (resources) cannot be assessed yet.

Leads with
A sentence that answers the question, then the checks that moved, grouped as worse and better.
Does well
The answer is the first thing read. A check that got worse while still failing (negative float) is caught as well as one that crossed its threshold. Each row has its own way in to the activities. All 14 are still on the page.
Costs
The checks are out of DCMA order, which is the order assessors know them by. "Worse" needs a rule for a check that fails in both updates. When nothing changed, the lead box is empty and the page becomes layout B.

What all three share

Not a choice here
No single score. Pass and fail are marked by a shape as well as a word, never by colour alone: warm colours mean late in Slip, and a failed check is not a late activity.
"See them" opens the changes grid showing the activities counted against that check. The engine already supports this.
Checks that cannot be assessed (resources) or are reported only (critical path test) say so in words and are never shown as passing.
The page says which dates the checks used: the ones stored in each file, as the summary does.
Open whichever is chosen
What "worse" means for a check that fails in both updates. The summary already uses: fails by more activities. A ratio check (the two indexes) has no activity count, so it needs its own rule.
