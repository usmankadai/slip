Update summary: three low-fidelity layouts

Hierarchy only: boxes and labels, no identity. The numbered tags show the order the eye should take. A heavy border marks what leads.

The planner's question: "What happened this month?"
Most prominent: the milestones that moved, each with a signed working-day delta, worst first.
Then: critical path joined and left; change counts by category; health delta.
Must not: become a dashboard of equal-weight tiles. Milestones lead.

Layouts drawn by Claude for Usman to choose between.

Costs
The strip below is three equal tiles, which is the start of the dashboard the spec warns against. With many moved milestones the strip falls below the fold and the secondary facts are not seen.

Costs
The milestone table is narrower, so less room for a before and after bar. The rail competes with the agent panel for the right-hand edge when that is open. Needs a stacked layout below about 1,000px.

Leads with
A written headline: the answer to "what happened this month" in the form the planner will have to give it to the client.
Does well
Furthest from a dashboard. Sets up the Finish update screen, which is also prose with evidence. Readable by someone who is not a planner.
Costs
A sentence is slower to scan than a table and hides the second-worst milestone. A template sentence reads stiffly in odd cases (nothing moved, forty things moved). Planners may not trust a sentence before they have seen the numbers.
